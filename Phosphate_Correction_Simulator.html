📖 Hypophosphatemia Protocols & References

          
        

        

          

            
1. Replacement Safety Limits

            
    
              
  • IV phosphate: 0.25–0.5 mmol/kg over 6–12 h for severe (<1.0 mg/dL) or symptomatic cases; lower doses (≈0.16–0.25 mmol/kg) for moderate IV replacement.
    • 
              
  • Rate: ≤7.5 mmol phosphate/h (≈1 mmol P = 31 mg elemental P). Dilute in 250–500 mL 0.9% saline or D5W; never give undiluted or as a bolus.
    • 
              
  • Salt choice: potassium phosphate if K⁺ is low (1.5 mmol K⁺ per mmol P; keep K⁺ ≤10 mmol/h); sodium phosphate if K⁺ is normal-high or in AKI.
    • 
              
  • eGFR <30 / AKI: halve the dose and recheck sooner — hyperphosphataemia risk.
    • 
              
  • Risks of overshoot: hypocalcaemia, calcium–phosphate precipitation, hypotension, hyperkalaemia (K salt).
    • 
            

          

          

            
2. Estimate Used Here

            

              
1 mmol phosphate = 31 mg elemental P; 1 mg/dL = 0.32 mmol/L

              
Serum rise ≈ 2–4 mg/dL per mmol/kg delivered

              
FEPO₄ = (UPO₄ × SCr) / (PPO₄ × UCr) × 100

            

            
Approximations only. Replace in aliquots and recheck rather than giving the whole estimate blindly.

          

          

            
3. Oral Replacement

            
Elemental phosphate about 1–2 g/day in divided doses (e.g. tablets of 250 mg ≈ 8 mmol). Diarrhoea is dose-limiting. Suited to mild or moderate cases with a working gut.

          

          

            
4. Refeeding Syndrome

            
In at-risk patients check phosphate, K⁺ and Mg²⁺ before feeding, give thiamine 100–300 mg IV first, start nutrition slowly, supplement electrolytes, and recheck every 12–24 h for the first 3 days.

          

          

            
5. Monitoring

            
    
              
  • Phosphate every 6 h during IV replacement; calcium, K⁺ and Mg²⁺ every 6–12 h.
    • 
              
  • ECG monitoring with potassium phosphate or any arrhythmia.
    • 
              
  • Phosphate commonly falls again as feeding continues — keep monitoring after correction.
    •

STEP 5 · HYPERPHOSPHATAEMIA

📖 Hyperphosphatemia Protocols & References

          
        

        

          

            
1. Severity & Urgency

            
    
              
  • Mild 4.6–5.9 · Moderate 6.0–9.9 · Severe ≥10 mg/dL.
    • 
              
  • Acute severe elevation (tumour lysis, rhabdomyolysis) can cause AKI through calcium–phosphate deposition in the kidney.
    • 
              
  • A Ca × P product above about 55 mg²/dL² risks soft-tissue calcification.
    • 
              
  • Low calcium with high phosphate: treat only if symptomatic, and with small doses.
    • 
            

          

          

            
2. Treatment

            

              
Acute, urine flowing: 0.9% saline 1–2 L, then 150–250 mL/h to urine output ≥100 mL/h; careful in heart failure. Treat the source (e.g. rasburicase and hydration in tumour lysis).

              
Binders with meals: sevelamer 800–1600 mg TDS or lanthanum 500–1000 mg TDS (chewed) are generally preferred; calcium acetate if calcium is low and not in tumour lysis. Short-term aluminium hydroxide only in severe acute cases (avoid long-term).

              
Dialysis: AKI or anuria with severe hyperphosphataemia, symptomatic hypocalcaemia, or tumour lysis refractory to fluids. CRRT removes phosphate steadily; intermittent HD is fast but rebounds 25–50%.

              
Diet: restrict to about 800–1000 mg phosphate/day (chronic).

            

          

          

            
3. Calcium Cautions

            
Avoid routine IV calcium while phosphate is high — it precipitates and worsens kidney injury. Give 1–2 g calcium gluconate over 10–20 min only for tetany, seizure, arrhythmia or marked QT prolongation, with ECG monitoring.

          

          

            
4. After Dialysis

            
Phosphate often rebounds after HD and can fall too low on prolonged CRRT — recheck at 2–6 h and replace phosphate if <2.5 mg/dL.

          

          

            
5. Monitoring

            
    
              
  • Phosphate, calcium, K⁺, creatinine and uric acid every 4–6 h in tumour lysis.
    • 
              
  • Urine output and fluid balance hourly during saline.
    • 
              
  • ECG if hyperkalaemia or hypocalcaemia is present.
    •
