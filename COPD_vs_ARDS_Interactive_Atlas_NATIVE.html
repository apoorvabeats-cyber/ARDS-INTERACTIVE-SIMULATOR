🩺 Step-by-Step Bedside Diagnostic Protocol
1
Inspiratory hold. Measure Pplat. High Ppeak with relatively preserved Pplat → increased resistance; high Ppeak + high Pplat → reduced compliance.
2
Expiratory hold. Auto-PEEP = Total PEEP − Set PEEP.
3
POCUS. Assess sliding, A/B-lines, pleural abnormalities, consolidations, diaphragm excursion and cardiac function.
4
Waveform review. Look for incomplete expiration, scooping, pressure abnormalities and patient effort.
5
Tailor ventilation. COPD → protect expiratory time; ARDS → lung protection and pressure limitation.
〰️ Flow-Time & Pressure-Time Waveforms

COPD — obstructive flow-time

Persistent failure to return to baseline before the next inspiration supports incomplete expiratory emptying and dynamic hyperinflation.

ARDS — flow-time

A clean return to zero indicates adequate emptying at that moment; ARDS diagnosis depends on the complete clinical picture.

Pressure-Time — high resistance

Pressure-Time — low compliance

⭕ Pressure-Volume & Flow-Volume Loops

Pressure–Volume: COPD / obstruction

A scooped/concave expiratory limb supports airflow obstruction.

Pressure–Volume: ARDS / low compliance

Flatter slope / less volume for a given pressure supports low compliance.

Flow–Volume: COPD

Concavity/scooping of the expiratory limb is a classic obstructive pattern.

Flow–Volume: restrictive pattern

Restrictive physiology generally reduces volumes; distinguish pulmonary restriction from acute ARDS using the full clinical context.

⚠️ Patient–Ventilator Dyssynchrony Waveforms

Ineffective triggering

Patient makes an inspiratory effort but no ventilator breath follows.

Look for: small pressure drop or flow deflection during expiration without a delivered breath. Auto-PEEP is an important contributor.

Double triggering

Two ventilator cycles occur from one sustained patient effort.

Look for: two consecutive breaths with little/no expiratory interval; assess excessive drive, inspiratory time and cycling.

Premature cycling

Ventilator switches to expiration before the patient's inspiratory effort has ended.

Look for: patient effort continuing into the expiratory phase; consider cycling criteria and inspiratory time.

Delayed cycling

Ventilator inspiration persists after the patient wants to exhale.

Look for: expiratory effort during the ventilator's inspiratory phase.

Flow starvation

Inspiratory demand exceeds delivered flow.

Look for: concave/scooped pressure-time inspiratory contour and increased patient effort.

Auto-PEEP / dynamic hyperinflation

Expiratory flow fails to reach zero before the next breath.

Think: excessive RR, long inspiratory time, airway obstruction or insufficient expiratory time.
🫁 Ventilatory Strategy by Phenotype

COPD-primary

Expiratory timeAvoid hyperinflation
  • Allow adequate expiratory time.
  • Consider lower RR when appropriate.
  • Use sufficient inspiratory flow to shorten inspiratory time.
  • Monitor auto-PEEP, triggering and hemodynamics.
  • External PEEP may help selected flow-limited patients; titrate cautiously.

ARDS-primary

Low VTPplat <30Lung protection
  • Use approximately 4–8 mL/kg predicted body weight, individualized.
  • Keep plateau pressure <30 cmH₂O.
  • Monitor driving pressure as a physiologic signal.
  • Use appropriate FiO₂/PEEP strategy according to severity and context.
🔀 Overlapping Phenotype — COPD + Secondary ARDS

High airway resistance + low compliance can coexist. Pneumonia, aspiration, sepsis or another secondary insult can produce ARDS in a patient with COPD.

High Raw

Treat obstruction and prevent dynamic hyperinflation.

Low Cstat

Use lung-protective ventilation and limit excessive pressure.

Priority: protect the injured lung while preserving adequate expiratory time and monitoring intrinsic PEEP.
📌 Quick Reference — One-Glance Differentiator

Resistance

COPD → HIGH
ARDS → usually normal/slightly ↑

Compliance

COPD → preserved/↑
ARDS → LOW

Auto-PEEP

COPD → common
ARDS → not defining

Flow

COPD → doesn't reach zero
ARDS → usually reaches zero

POCUS

COPD → A-lines / hyperinflation clues
ARDS → patchy B-lines / pleural abnormalities

Primary failure

COPD → hypercapnia
ARDS → hypoxemia
