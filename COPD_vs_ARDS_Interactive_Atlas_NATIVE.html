🩺 Step-by-Step Bedside Diagnostic Protocol
1
Inspiratory hold. Measure Pplat. High Ppeak with relatively preserved Pplat → increased resistance; high Ppeak + high Pplat → reduced compliance.
2
Expiratory hold. Auto-PEEP = Total PEEP − Set PEEP.
3
POCUS. Assess sliding, A/B-lines, pleural abnormalities, consolidations, diaphragm excursion and cardiac function.
4
Waveform review. Look for incomplete expiration, scooping, pressure abnormalities and patient effort.
5
Tailor ventilation. COPD: oxygen 88–92%, then expiratory time. ARDS: tidal volume about 6 mL/kg, plateau ≤30, driving pressure ≤15.
〰️ Pressure, flow, and volume

Same scales for both. COPD: a wide peak-to-plateau gap, expiratory flow still negative, volume still up. ARDS: peak and plateau are both high, and flow and volume get back to the baseline.

⭕ Pressure-Volume & Flow-Volume Loops

Pressure–Volume: COPD / obstruction

Auto-PEEP keeps the loop from closing on set PEEP. The scooped expiratory limb is the flow–volume loop below, not this one.

Pressure–Volume: ARDS / low compliance

Flatter slope / less volume for a given pressure supports low compliance.

Flow–Volume: COPD

Concavity/scooping of the expiratory limb is a classic obstructive pattern.

Flow–Volume: restrictive pattern

Restrictive physiology generally reduces volumes; distinguish pulmonary restriction from acute ARDS using the full clinical context.

⚠️ Patient–Ventilator Dyssynchrony Waveforms

Ineffective triggering

Patient makes an inspiratory effort but no ventilator breath follows.

Look for: small pressure drop or flow deflection during expiration without a delivered breath. Auto-PEEP is an important contributor.

Double triggering

Two ventilator cycles occur from one sustained patient effort.

Look for: two consecutive breaths with little/no expiratory interval; assess excessive drive, inspiratory time and cycling.

Premature cycling

Ventilator switches to expiration before the patient's inspiratory effort has ended.

Look for: patient effort continuing into the expiratory phase; consider cycling criteria and inspiratory time.

Delayed cycling

Ventilator inspiration persists after the patient wants to exhale.

Look for: expiratory effort during the ventilator's inspiratory phase.

Flow starvation

Inspiratory demand exceeds delivered flow.

Look for: concave inspiratory pressure. More often the high-drive ARDS patient than the obstructed one.

Auto-PEEP

Expiratory flow is still negative when the next breath starts.

Cause: rate too high, inspiratory time too long, or obstructed airways. This is the usual reason a COPD effort does not trigger.

Which patient: an ineffective trigger is the usual COPD miss. Double triggering and flow starvation are more often ARDS with a strong drive.

🫁 Ventilatory Strategy by Phenotype

COPD-primary

SpO₂ 88–92%Long expiration
  • Controlled oxygen to 88–92%. A higher saturation raises PaCO₂.
  • Bronchodilator and a steroid before the ventilator.
  • NIV if the pH stays under 7.35 and the patient can protect the airway.
  • If intubated: low rate, high inspiratory flow, I:E 1:4 or 1:5. Accept a high PaCO₂.
  • A falling blood pressure is trapped gas. Let the chest empty. Do not give a fluid bolus for it.
  • External PEEP, if used to help triggering, stays below measured auto-PEEP.

ARDS-primary

Vt about 6Pplat ≤30ΔP ≤15
  • Tidal volume about 6 mL/kg predicted body weight, and always 4–8.
  • Plateau ≤30. Driving pressure ≤15, using total PEEP.
  • Pair FiO₂ with PEEP on the lower ARDSNet table. Target SpO₂ 88–95%.
  • If P/F stays ≤150 on FiO₂ ≥0.60 and PEEP ≥5, prone for at least 16 hours.
  • No routine recruitment and no routine paralysis. A corticosteroid does not replace the tidal volume.
🔀 Overlapping Phenotype — COPD + Secondary ARDS

High airway resistance + low compliance can coexist. Pneumonia, aspiration, sepsis or another secondary insult can produce ARDS in a patient with COPD.

High Raw

Treat obstruction and prevent dynamic hyperinflation.

Low Cstat

Use lung-protective ventilation and limit excessive pressure.

Priority: keep the tidal volume lung-protective and do not raise the rate to chase carbon dioxide. High PEEP that is right for ARDS can worsen auto-PEEP and drop the blood pressure.
📌 Quick Reference — One-Glance Differentiator

Resistance

COPD → HIGH
ARDS → usually normal/slightly ↑

Compliance

COPD → preserved/↑
ARDS → LOW

Auto-PEEP

COPD → common
ARDS → not defining

Flow

COPD → doesn't reach zero
ARDS → usually reaches zero

POCUS

A-lines do not prove COPD
ARDS: patchy B-lines, irregular pleura, spared areas

Primary failure

COPD → hypercapnia
ARDS → hypoxemia
🧮 Bedside Formula Reference

Auto-PEEP

Total PEEP − Set PEEP

Driving pressure

Pplat − total PEEP

Total PEEP is set PEEP plus auto-PEEP. Using set PEEP alone is wrong when gas is trapped.

Peak–plateau gap

Ppeak − Pplat

A wide gap is resistance. COPD is wide. ARDS is narrow, with both numbers high.

Static compliance

VT ÷ (Pplat − total PEEP)

Same total PEEP as the driving pressure. Low in ARDS. Often preserved in COPD until hyperinflation.
