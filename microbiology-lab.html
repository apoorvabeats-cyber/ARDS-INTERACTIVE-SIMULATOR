    Scientific notes and limits of this model
    • NDM is a zinc-dependent metallo-β-lactamase found in the periplasm of Gram-negative bacteria. It is not a porin and it does not pump antibiotics out: it hydrolyses susceptible β-lactams. Zn²⁺ ions activate a water molecule (hydroxide) that attacks the β-lactam carbonyl.
    • Real NDM-producing Pseudomonas can also carry efflux, porin loss, AmpC and other mechanisms. They are all switched off here.
    • Structures are schematic and not to scale (1 unit ≈ 0.25 nm; the periplasm is compressed). Zn²⁺ and water are enlarged for visibility.
    • Resistance Lab. Ticked mechanisms are animated as parallel pathways of different drug molecules (never entered, pumped out, hydrolysed, reached the target with weak binding, bound), never as one molecule with incompatible fates. The percentages come from transparent teaching weights per organism and drug (for example OprD loss matters most for imipenem, MexAB-OprM extrudes meropenem but not imipenem, ESBLs spare carbapenems). They are not MICs and not a clinical antibiogram.
    • Target alteration is shown as a separate, hypothetical mechanism, not assumed for every isolate. Only 6 drug molecules are drawn; other copies of enzymes and PBPs exist but are not drawn. Cephalosporins use a generic schematic cephalosporin core.
    • So far the Resistance Lab covers four Gram-negative organisms (Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Escherichia coli, Acinetobacter baumannii). Gram-positives (MRSA, VRE), anaerobes and fungi are not built yet.
    • Educational model written for teaching, not for treatment decisions. Susceptibility must come from the isolate’s antimicrobial susceptibility testing.
