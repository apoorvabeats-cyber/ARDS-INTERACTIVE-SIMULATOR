📖 Hypomagnesemia Protocols & References

          
        

        

          

            
1. Replacement Safety Limits

            
    
              
  • Planned IV infusion: MgSO₄ at ≤1 g/h (4% = 40 mg/mL or more dilute). Slower infusion loses less in urine.
    • 
              
  • Emergency (torsades, seizure): 2 g IV over 5–15 min, then a planned infusion. Pulseless torsades: 1–2 g IV push.
    • 
              
  • eGFR <30 / AKI / oliguria: halve the dose and recheck the level before repeating — hypermagnesaemia risk.
    • 
              
  • Stop if patellar reflexes fade, RR <12, hypotension or urine output <30 mL/h.
    • 
            

          

          

            
2. Deficit Estimate Used Here

            

              
1 g MgSO₄·7H₂O = 8.1 mEq = 4.06 mmol ≈ 98.6 mg elemental Mg

              
Serum rise ≈ 0.08–0.15 mg/dL per g retained

              
FEMg = (UMg × SCr) / (0.7 × PMg × UCr) × 100

            

            
Serum Mg²⁺ reflects <1% of total body Mg²⁺, so these are approximations. About half of an IV dose is excreted within hours; stores take days to refill — continue replacement for 1–2 days after the level normalises.

          

          

            
3. Oral Magnesium

            
Only for mild, asymptomatic cases with a working gut. Absorption is ~30% and diarrhoea limits the dose; organic salts (glycinate, citrate, lactate) are often better tolerated than oxide.

          

          

            
4. Refractory Hypokalaemia / Hypocalcaemia

            
Low Mg²⁺ causes renal K⁺ wasting and PTH resistance. Potassium and calcium will often not correct until Mg²⁺ is replaced — give it concurrently.

          

          

            
5. Monitoring

            
    
              
  • Check Mg²⁺ about 6 h after an infusion ends (immediate levels overestimate), then daily.
    • 
              
  • Continuous ECG if arrhythmia or QT prolongation; keep K⁺ ≥4.0 and avoid QT-prolonging drugs.
    • 
              
  • Check reflexes, respiratory rate and BP during infusion, especially with renal impairment.
    •

📖 Hypermagnesemia Protocols & References

          
        

        

          

            
1. Level vs Clinical Picture (approximate)

            
    
              
  • 2.6–4.9 mg/dL: usually silent; nausea, flushing, weakness.
    • 
              
  • 5–8.9: hypotension, drowsiness, reduced reflexes.
    • 
              
  • ≥9: areflexia, respiratory depression, long PR / wide QRS, bradycardia.
    • 
              
  • ≥12–15: paralysis, complete heart block, cardiac arrest.
    • 
              
  • Pre-eclampsia therapeutic range is about 4.8–8.4 mg/dL — stop the infusion if reflexes are lost, RR <12 or urine output <30 mL/h.
    • 
            

          

          

            
2. Treatment Order

            

              
Stop sources: MgSO₄, antacids, laxatives, enemas, Epsom salts, Mg-containing TPN.

              
Antagonise: 10% calcium gluconate 10–20 mL (1–2 g) IV over ~10 min; over 3 min if arrest or eclamptic toxicity. Repeat as needed. Effect lasts 30–60 min.

              
Support: ventilate early if respiratory depression; pace for symptomatic bradycardia; treat hypotension.

              
Clear: 0.9% saline 1–2 L (if not overloaded) + furosemide 20–40 mg IV with adequate renal function; replace urine losses (K⁺, Ca²⁺ are wasted too).

              
Dialyse: renal failure, anuria, level ≥9–12 with symptoms, ECG changes, or refractory.

            

          

          

            
3. Dialysis Notes

            
Haemodialysis removes magnesium rapidly; use a low- or Mg-free dialysate. Expect a rebound of roughly 10–25% afterwards — recheck at 2–4 h.

          

          

            
4. Monitoring

            
    
              
  • Continuous ECG and SpO₂; reflexes and RR every 30–60 min until improving.
    • 
              
  • Mg²⁺ at 1–2 h, 4 h, 6 h, then every 6–12 h until normal and stable.
    • 
              
  • Watch K⁺ and Ca²⁺ during forced diuresis.
    •
