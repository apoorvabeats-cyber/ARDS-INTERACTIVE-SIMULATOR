Rescue therapies — names to know
  • Prone positioning: prolonged sessions, especially when P/F ≤150.
  • Inhaled pulmonary vasodilator: iNO or inhaled epoprostenol as a temporary rescue/bridge for refractory hypoxemia.
  • Neuromuscular blockade: selected patients with severe dyssynchrony or inability to achieve protective ventilation; not routine for every patient.
  • VV-ECMO: consider referral for refractory severe hypoxemia/hypercapnic acidosis despite optimized management.
  • Recruitment maneuvers: not routine; use only selectively and cautiously because of hemodynamic/overdistension risk.
Clinical Decision Tree
⚠️ Persistent severe hypoxemiaP/F 78. Review lung-protective ventilation and consider prone positioning.
ARDS severitySevere

1. Oxygenation

Target SpO₂ 88–95%. If improving, reduce FiO₂ stepwise first.

2. Lung protection / PEEP

Maintain Vt 4–8 mL/kg PBW and Pplat <30; after FiO₂ is low, reassess PEEP.

3. Prone positioning

For P/F ≤150, consider prolonged prone positioning (typically ≥16 h/session).

4. Liberation / rescue

If refractory: rescue/ECMO pathway. If improving with low FiO₂/PEEP: assess SBT/extubation readiness.
