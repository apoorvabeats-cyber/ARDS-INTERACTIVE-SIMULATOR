2. Clinical Grading

WFNS scale

World Federation of Neurosurgical Societies grade. It uses the Glasgow Coma Scale plus a motor deficit. It is not the Hunt–Hess scale.

IGCS 15, no motor deficit
IIGCS 13–14, no motor deficit
IIIGCS 13–14, with a motor deficit
IVGCS 7–12, deficit or not
VGCS 3–6, deficit or not

A motor deficit means hemiparesis, hemiplegia, or aphasia. An isolated cranial-nerve palsy, such as a third-nerve palsy, does not raise the grade. GCS uses the best limb, so a patient can be GCS 15 and still have weakness on the other side. That combination is not in the original table. Do not force it into grade I.

Hunt–Hess scale

This is a clinical description. It does not use the GCS. Report the worst feature.

IAlert. Mild headache or none. No deficit.
IISevere headache or a cranial-nerve palsy. Alert.
IIIDrowsy or confused, or a mild focal deficit.
IVStupor, or moderate to severe hemiparesis.
VComa, decerebrate, or moribund.

The 1968 description moves the patient one grade worse for serious systemic disease. Say the clinical grade first, then the adjustment. A third-nerve palsy makes this grade II, not WFNS III.

4. Pre-Aneurysm-Securing BP

Principles

  • Avoid severe hypertension and large BP variability.
  • Avoid hypotension and compromised cerebral perfusion.
  • Use a titratable IV strategy when clinically indicated.
  • After aneurysm securing, BP goals become individualized according to neurological status and complications such as DCI.

5. Aneurysm Treatment

Key Principle

Do not delay definitive treatment unnecessarily.
Once the ruptured aneurysm is identified, prompt treatment is central to reducing the risk of rebleeding. The 2023 AHA/ASA guideline recommends treatment preferably within 24 hours when feasible.

6. Delayed Cerebral Ischemia

New focal neurological deficit
Unexplained reduction in consciousness
Imaging suggests cerebral vasospasm
Euvolemia maintained

Prevention / Management Principles

Nimodipine: 60 mg enterally every 4 hours for 21 days, started early. If the blood pressure falls, give 30 mg every 2 hours. Do not stop it as the first move.
Routine prophylactic hypervolemia is not recommended. Maintain euvolemia and monitor closely for neurological deterioration.
For symptomatic DCI, after the aneurysm is secured and other causes are excluded, induced hypertension may be used under neurocritical-care supervision. Do not raise the blood pressure while the aneurysm is still open.

7. Neurocritical-Care Bundle

Nimodipine started
Frequent neurological assessment
Euvolemia / hemodynamic monitoring
Hydrocephalus assessed
EEG considered when clinically appropriate
VTE prevention strategy addressed
Early rehabilitation / recovery planning

Hydrocephalus & Seizures

Acute symptomatic hydrocephalus requires urgent neurocritical and neurosurgical assessment and CSF diversion when indicated.

New-onset seizures after aSAH are treated when present. Routine prophylactic antiseizure medication is not recommended for every patient, but selected high-risk patients may warrant consideration.

Phenytoin should generally be avoided for seizure prophylaxis because of its association with excess morbidity.

Do not give routine tranexamic acid. It does not improve outcome after aneurysmal SAH.

Hyponatremia is common. Do not fluid-restrict. Distinguish SIADH from cerebral salt wasting and keep the patient euvolemic.
