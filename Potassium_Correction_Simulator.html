📖 Hypokalemia Protocols & References

          
        

        

          

            
1. Replacement Safety Limits

            
    
              
  • Peripheral IV: ≤10 mmol/h and ≤40 mmol/L (vein irritation, pain).
    • 
              
  • Central IV: usually 10–20 mmol/h with continuous ECG; up to 40 mmol/h only for life-threatening arrhythmia, in ICU, with hourly K⁺.
    • 
              
  • Never give IV KCl as a bolus or push. Concentrated KCl only via central line, via a pump.
    • 
              
  • Use 0.9% saline as carrier — dextrose causes insulin release and shifts K⁺ into cells.
    • 
              
  • eGFR <30 / AKI / oliguria: halve rates and recheck sooner (rebound hyperkalaemia risk).
    • 
            

          

          

            
2. Deficit Estimate Used Here

            

              
K⁺ 3.0–4.0: deficit ≈ 100–200 mmol per 1 mmol/L fall

              
K⁺ <3.0: ≈ 200–400 mmol more per additional 1 mmol/L fall

              
Rule of thumb: 10 mmol KCl ≈ +0.1 mmol/L serum K⁺

            

            
These are bedside approximations. Replace in aliquots (e.g. 40 mmol) and recheck rather than giving the whole calculated deficit blindly. 15% KCl 10 mL ampoule ≈ 20 mmol K⁺.

          

          

            
3. Magnesium

            
Hypokalaemia is often refractory until Mg²⁺ is replaced. For Mg²⁺ <1.7 mg/dL give MgSO₄ 2 g IV over 1–2 h (over 5–15 min if torsades / VT).

          

          

            
4. Salt Choice

            
KCl is the default. Use K-bicarbonate / citrate with metabolic acidosis (RTA, diarrhoea) and K-phosphate when phosphate is also low (e.g. DKA recovery).

          

          

            
5. Monitoring

            
    
              
  • IV ≥10 mmol/h or K⁺ <2.5: continuous ECG; K⁺ every 1–2 h.
    • 
              
  • Stable IV replacement: K⁺ 4–6 hourly. Oral: recheck within 12–24 h.
    • 
              
  • Transcellular shift (thyrotoxic paralysis, insulin, β-agonist): use smaller doses — rebound hyperkalaemia once the shift reverses.
    •

📖 Hyperkalemia Protocols & References

          
        

        

          

            
1. Severity & Urgency

            
    
              
  • Mild 5.5–5.9 · Moderate 6.0–6.4 · Severe ≥6.5 mmol/L.
    • 
              
  • Any ECG change (peaked T, flat P, wide QRS, sine wave) = emergency, whatever the K⁺ value.
    • 
              
  • AKI, oliguria, ongoing K⁺ release, acidosis or digoxin raise the risk — treat at a lower threshold.
    • 
            

          

          

            
2. Three Prongs of Treatment

            

              
Stabilise: 10% calcium gluconate 30 mL (or 10% calcium chloride 10 mL, central preferred) IV over 5–10 min; repeat after 5–10 min if ECG changes persist. Onset in minutes, lasts 30–60 min. Don't mix with bicarbonate.

              
Shift: regular insulin 10 U IV (5 U if renal impairment or low weight) + 25 g glucose; omit glucose if baseline ≥250 mg/dL. Salbutamol 10–20 mg nebulised. Bicarbonate only if acidotic.

              
Remove: loop diuretic if urine output; K⁺ binder (SZC 10 g up to TID); dialysis for severe, refractory, anuric or ongoing release.

            

          

          

            
3. Hypoglycaemia After Insulin

            
Check glucose at 30 and 60 min, then hourly for at least 6 h — risk is highest with renal impairment, low baseline glucose and no dextrose infusion afterwards.

          

          

            
4. Pseudohyperkalaemia

            
Haemolysis, fist clenching, delayed processing, very high platelets or WBC. Confirm with a whole-blood gas K⁺ when the patient is well, has no ECG changes and no risk factors — but never delay treatment if the ECG is abnormal.

          

          

            
5. Monitoring

            
    
              
  • Continuous ECG while K⁺ ≥6.0 or ECG abnormal.
    • 
              
  • Recheck K⁺ at 1 h, 2 h, 4 h and 6 h, then every 6 h for 24 h to catch rebound.
    • 
              
  • After dialysis or large shift therapy, watch for over-correction to hypokalaemia.
    •
