MASTER CNS INFECTION PATHWAY

1. SUSPECT CNS INFECTION Fever ± headache, neck stiffness, altered sensorium, seizure, focal neurological deficit, behavioural change, cranial neuropathy, photophobia, vomiting or features of raised intracranial pressure.
↓
2. IMMEDIATE ICU ASSESSMENT ABC • SpO₂ • ECG • BP • temperature • GCS • pupils • bedside glucose • IV access ×2 • CBC • electrolytes • renal/liver function • lactate when septic.

Obtain blood cultures ×2
↓
3. IDENTIFY IMMEDIATE THREATS Airway failure → secure airway
Shock → resuscitation + vasopressor
Status epilepticus → seizure pathway
Raised ICP/herniation → ICP pathway
↓
4. DEFINE THE CLINICAL SYNDROME Meningitis • Encephalitis • Meningoencephalitis • Brain abscess • Ventriculitis • TB meningitis • Cryptococcal meningitis • Opportunistic CNS infection • Spinal epidural abscess.
↓
5. DO NOT DELAY EMPIRIC THERAPY When bacterial meningitis or encephalitis is strongly suspected, obtain cultures promptly and start appropriate empiric treatment. Do not allow CT or LP delays to postpone life-saving therapy.
